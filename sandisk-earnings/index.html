Verdict: SNDK earnings results beat — and guidance raised

This was not a narrow beat. SanDisk cleared its own guidance and Wall Street consensus at the same time, then guided the next quarter materially higher.

On the beat: Revenue came in at $11.031 billion, above the top end of the $10.3 billion–$10.8 billion range SanDisk had guided. Non-GAAP EPS of $49.51 was above the company’s $44.00–$46.00 guide and about $3.28 ahead of consensus near $46.23. Gross margin — the line most watched for signs of a NAND cycle peak — printed at 85.1%, above the guided range rather than rolling over.

On the raise: Fiscal Q2 guidance calls for another sequential step-up to $12.2 billion–$12.6 billion in revenue and $55.00–$57.00 in non-GAAP EPS. That matters because last quarter’s debate was whether growth would taper after Q1. Instead, the midpoint of the new guide implies roughly 12% sequential revenue growth and roughly 13% sequential EPS growth from Q1’s already-beaten numbers.

On quality of growth: About 60% of the sequential revenue increase came from volume and about 40% from pricing, according to the earnings deck — a healthier mix than a purely price-driven quarter. Cash generation backed it up: operating cash flow was $6.363 billion and adjusted free cash flow was $5.696 billion, with non-GAAP operating income of $8.871 billion.

The honest caveat: Consumer was weak — down 29% sequentially and 39% year over year to $397 million — and margin guidance for Q2 (84.5%–85.5%) is essentially flat versus Q1, not expanding. The beat-and-raise verdict rests on Datacenter strength and the Q2 outlook, not on every segment firing at once.

Why Datacenter mattered most

Datacenter revenue rose from $2.977 billion to $4.802 billion in one quarter — a $1.8 billion sequential jump that alone accounts for most of SanDisk’s $2.07 billion total revenue increase. That is why the segment led the verdict: it shows the growth is tied to AI data-center build-outs and enterprise SSD demand, not only to NAND pricing. Management expects Datacenter to exceed 55% of the total NAND market in calendar 2027, with Datacenter bit demand up about 40% year over year — a backdrop that, if it holds, supports the raised outlook.

The Consumer warning sign

Consumer fell to $397 million, down 29% sequentially and 39% year over year. It is now under 4% of total revenue, so it does not change this quarter’s headline — but it is the clearest evidence that end demand outside AI infrastructure is soft, and it is the segment to watch for any broadening slowdown.

Growth mix: Roughly 60% of sequential growth came from volume (more bits shipped) and 40% from pricing. Volume-led growth is generally read as more durable than price-only growth in a memory cycle.

Why the guidance mattered: Heading into the print, the central fear was a guide-down — that Q1’s strength was a peak and management would signal a taper. The opposite happened. The Q2 revenue midpoint of $12.4 billion is about 12% above Q1’s actual revenue and well above the level implied by Q1 consensus, while the EPS midpoint of $56 is about 13% above Q1’s actual EPS. Margin guidance of 84.5%–85.5% also signals no near-term margin collapse, holding essentially at Q1’s 85.1% level.

Market outlook from management: SanDisk expects a strong NAND market in calendar 2027, with Datacenter representing over 55% of the market and Datacenter bit demand rising about 40% year over year in 2027.

What to watch next after SNDK earnings

  • The earnings call commentary (held Oct 29, 1:30 p.m. PT) — detail on NBM customer demand, NAND pricing durability, and whether Datacenter orders extend into late fiscal 2027.
  • Consumer softness — whether the −29% sequential drop stabilizes or spreads; Consumer is small today, but it is the canary for non-AI demand.
  • Margin delivery vs. the 84.5%–85.5% Q2 guide — holding margins near 85% is central to the beat-and-raise story; a slip below the range would change the narrative.
  • Split-adjusted trading from November 20, 2026 — per-share figures will be restated on a split-adjusted basis after the 8-for-1 split.
  • NBM conversion — how quickly the $93.9B contracted floor and $88.9B RPO translate into recognized revenue across fiscal 2027–2028.

SanDisk earnings FAQ

Did SanDisk beat earnings in Q1 FY2027?

Yes. Revenue of $11.031B beat consensus of about $10.64B and the top of company guidance, non-GAAP EPS of $49.51 beat consensus of about $46.23, and non-GAAP gross margin of 85.1% beat the 83%–85% guided range.

What is SanDisk’s Q2 FY2027 guidance?

Revenue of $12.2B–$12.6B, non-GAAP EPS of $55.00–$57.00 (pre-split), and non-GAAP gross margin of 84.5%–85.5%, with non-GAAP operating expenses of $540M–$560M and about 151 million diluted shares.

How much did Datacenter revenue grow?

Datacenter revenue was $4.802B in Q1 FY2027, up 61% sequentially from $2.977B in Q4 FY2026 and up 1,682% year over year.

When is the SanDisk stock split?

The 8-for-1 forward split’s additional shares are expected to be distributed November 19, 2026, with split-adjusted trading beginning November 20, 2026.

Which segment was weak?

Consumer — revenue of $397M, down 29% sequentially and 39% year over year. Edge grew 7% sequentially to $5.8B and Datacenter grew 61% sequentially.
